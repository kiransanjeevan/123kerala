Dilip-Mohanlal: StarWars?

Dilip whose star is shining currently at the Kerala box office has been banned for five years from acting in films by the Kerala Film Chamber. A fine of Rs 20 lakhs has been imposed on him and an apology has been demanded. The reason being cited is that Dilip was given a cheque for Rs 1.90 lakhs by distributor Dinesh Panicker for the film 'Udayapuram Sultan'. The cheque bounced and Dilip filed a case. Panicker did not reply or respond to the court summons. He was arrested and produced in court. He was then remanded to judicial custody, but shifted to a hospital on medical grounds. The Distributors and the Producers lobby working against Dilip took up the issue and got the trade bodies to issue the ban on Dilip.

In an earlier confrontation between film artistes and the Chamber it was decided that no trade body would issue any public statement until May 30, since MACTA the technicians body had agreed to mediate and solve the issue. Due to this, the Chamber did not come out with any statement on the ban. AMMA the Artistes Association has made it clear that if the reports on the ban on Dilip were true, then they would take Dilip's side. Panciker's case was only a ruse to cut Dilip down to size. The South Kerala lobby which backs Mohanlal is supposed to be behind this ban. Most of Dilip's producers are from North Kerala and the directors who signed him when he was a nobody are getting preference. The new Dilip lobby has trampled upon the forces that back the ageing superstars.

Looks like the war is now hotting up. Dilip's effigies were burnt in various parts of Kerala by Lal fans. Mohanlal called Dilip, and asked him to remain calm. But it is an open secret that the two lobbies are actively working against each other. Dilip is backed by script writer-director Lohitadas and producer Panicker's supporters are the Lal camp followers in South Kerala.

Panicker and Unni who were joint producers of 'Kirredom' the Mohanlal-starrer, later split up. Unni was the first to produce a film with Dilip. Panicker chose to make quickies with lesser known artistes. His way of working was to bankroll producers who came to him and offered satellite rights. One such case was 'Udaypuram Sultan' starring Dilip. He had struck a deal with Dilip saying that he would issue a cheque for Rs 1.90 lakhs as a guarantee in case the producer backed out. The producer's cheque bounced and Dilip banked on Panicker whose cheque also bounced. Panicker did not bother to clear up matters since he thought Dilip would take up the matter with the producer and not with him, who was only a distributor. It was here that he made his mistake.

Unni, his one time partner, instigated Dilip to press charges and got an arrest warrant. Using his proximity to Congress ministers in Tiruananthapuram, Unni got the police to arrest Panicker on May 24 which was a holiday for Nabi, followed by two more holidays. Panicker was dragged out by the cops from his residence and taken in Dilip's vehicle to Alwaye where he was produced before the magistrate and remanded to custody. Bail application could be moved only after the court opened.

Panicker's situation was immediately taken up by the Mohanlal lobby. They got the distributor-exhibitor-producer lobby to meet in Kottayam and issue a ban. Lal and Mammootty were in Tiruananthapuram to oversee the operations but officially they were there to discuss the chamber ban on film artistes appearing on TV. Lal's fans then swung into action. For their part, Dilip's lobby got a huge crowd to gather at the airport to welcome the actor. Dilip fans shouted anti-chamber slogans. When 'Chakram' the film which was to be directed by Kamal was shelved, the blame was put on Lal. It was said that Lal wanted Lohitadas to rewrite the story and cut Dilip's role to size. It could not be a coincidence that Lohitadas was seen at the forefront of the pro-Dilip supporters.

Dilip has stated that he would withdraw the case if AMMA, the Artistes' Association told him to do so and took on the responsibility of getting him his money. Star wars and that too proxy wars in Kerala are hotting up. Let's wait for more action.
