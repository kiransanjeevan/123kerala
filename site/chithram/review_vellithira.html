Review: Vellithira

Director Bhadran has tried to give Pritviraj an action hero image through Vellithira, which is a slick action packed films like his earlier Spadikam and Olympian Antony Adam. But Pritviraj has sadly failed to live up to the expectation of the Kerala audience of becoming a mass hero.

Style Raj (Pritviraj) comes to a remote village in Attapadi in the midst of a jungle with a mobile projector along with his two friends and starts a touring talkie. They show Pulikodu Valiban a medley of Rajnikanth hits and make a living out of it. They use the three-phase power of a rice mill owner to run their projector and slowly his daughter Pankajam (Kalpana) falls in love with Raj. Raj encourages her so that he gets uninterrupted power supply, but he falls for a village belle, Thatha (Navya Nair), a blacksmith (Kalabhavan Mani) sister. But Raj has a dark secret, as he is on parole from jail for a life imprisonment. His intention was to make enough money for his sister’s education and then go back to jail. But soon police tracks him down and he escapes once again to Bangalore. In a tame climax, the lovers are united and Raj goes back to jail.

The major fault with "Vellithira" is an absence of a good story. The story is a rehash of old films like Picnic and the climax is a major let down. The film was made to showcase a new action hero but there is no peg to hang the story. Pritviraj has to improve on his dialogue delivery while Navya Nair lacks glamour essential ingredient in such kind of roles. A comic twist is added in the form of a Panchayat president (Cochin Haneefa) who supports a rival permanent theatre owner Jose (Jagathy), who runs soft porn films. There is a conflict between Raj and Jose, which leads to a lot of laughter. The songs tuned by debutant Alphone are picturised colourfully and are fast paced. The camera of S.Kumar has etched the scenic beauty of Wynad beautifully. On the whole Vellithira fails to impress.
