Review: Panthayakozhi

 
Award winning director M.A Venu's first commercial mass masala Panthya Kozhi with Narain as an action hero and produced by the leading production house Lal Creations is a mixed bag. It is a decent entertainer but the story and presentation is as old as the hills. The film is about a young man, Nandagopal (Narain), bound by responsibilities which makes him unable to chase even his limited dreams. He lives with his mother (Geetha) and mute sister (Remya Nambeesan) at the mercy of his uncle (Janaradhanan). They come to know through their family friend Mustafa (Madhu), that their late father (Lal) a forest officer had a bungalow in Kalimuthupalayam, a Tamil Nadu village.

Nandagopal along with his friends (Sudhish, Srijit Ravi and Anoop) go to Kalimuthupalayam in a desperate effort to sell the bungalow which they think is a great chance to get over their financial difficulties. Things are not easy for them as they encounter Gounder (Rami Reddy) the villain who refuses to vacate the house. Nandagopal also falls in love with a village girl Chembakam (Pooja) which complicates matter further.

If you think that the story has come on screen several times earlier, there lies the problem with Panthayakozhi. Even the villain, a typical Gounder, who in our films are always the rich, brash, rural chieftains and has the whole village waiting for his commands, seems to have come straight out of some old flicks. Again, the mother clad in a white saree who can prompt the hero to thrash the baddies with some hard hitting dialogues remind you of Rakhee or Nirupa Roy doing the same in umpteen Bollywood films.

With item numbers becoming an accepted ingredient in Malayalam films these days, it could be natural for the hero to rush to a dance bar, when he is in anguish! There are four songs in the film, tuned by Alex Paul with Karimbanude Naatil… being the pick of the lot. Pallaseri has rehashed some of his earlier dialogues. Narain as Nandagopal is terrific, and has the potential to be a full fledged action hero. Pooja has hardly any chance to emote other than looking good in songs.

Panthayakozhi is time-pass masala entertainment. To be fair to the makers of the film, they seem to have made an attempt to try out Narain as an action hero in a revenge drama, but sadly there is nothing new in story and presentation.
