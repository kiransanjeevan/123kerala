Are Kerala Marxists junking their ideology?

Downsizing and retrenchment are no longer an anathema to the Communist Party of India - Marxist, though it has been opposing the Central government's economic liberalisation policies over the last few years.

Ousted from power after the Kerala assembly elections in May, the CPI-M has come to realise the new economy truth that employees of a company that does not make profit should be sacked.

So, declining profits and an inner party power struggle have forced Kairali TV, the CPI-M-owned television channel in Kerala, to sack 18 of its employees.

But a fortnight after Kairali laid off the employees that included the channel's executive director P A Sidhartha Menon and programme director P T Kunjumohammad, a power struggle is on within the party to control the television company, Malayalam Communications Ltd.

The infighting in the party is between senior CPI-M functionary and leader of the opposition in the Kerala assembly V S Achuthanandan and general secretary Pinarayi Vijayan. CPI-M insiders claim that those sacked from Kairali were the supporters of Achuthanandan.

However, Achuthanandan said, "It is ridiculous to claim that there is a power struggle within our party and that led to the retrenchment of the employees in Kairali TV. Private companies often undertake restructuring," Achuthanandan told.

He said the people who do not know the basics of how a company operates are spreading rumours that the CPI-M is indulging in anti-labour practices.

"In fact, it is wrong to say that Kairali TV is owned by the CPI-M. The party had only helped promote the channel. We do not know anything about its daily operations," he added.

Mammootty, matinee idol and Kairali's chairman, refused to comment on the issue. But dismissed employees -- most of them journalists -- are on a war path.

CPI-M leaders revealed that one of the main reasons for the downsizing of Kairali TV, launched with much fanfare last year, is because the "government advertisement revenues have dried up".

Till it was ousted from power in May, the CPI-M government in Kerala had been liberally releasing advertisements to the television channel. But after Chief Minister A K Antony came to power, the Congress government stopped giving advertisements to it.

Kairali, which has an equity base of Rs 584.5 million, has as its shareholders the CPI-M, many of the party's affiliated organisations and businessmen sympathetic to the party. The company made a net loss of around Rs 80 million last year.

As journalists and other employees demand justice from the TV channel, the retrenchment issue has become an acute embarrassment for the CPI-M.

The Congress government also did not lose the opportunity to criticise the CPI-M's "capitalistic" mentality.

According to state Minister for Labour Babu Divakaran, the CPI-M has now woken up to reality. "We are happy that the Marxists in Kerala have now realised that if a company has to survive, hard decisions should be taken," he told.

"These CPI-M leaders were the same people who have been fiercely opposing downsizing and restructuring of corporates and public sector undertakings. Where has their ideology gone now?" Divakaran asked.
