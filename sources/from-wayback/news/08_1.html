Govt sacks 8 as staff stir continues

THIRUVANANTHAPURAM: The Kerala government cracked the whip and terminated eight workers, as a paralysing strike by half a million of its employees, belonging to unions owing allegiance to ruling and Opposition fronts, entered the second day on Thursday.

Employees took to the streets here while national trade union leaders poured in from New Delhi, protesting against the government's cost cutting measures announced by Chief Minister A K Antony. Facing one of its worst financial crisis, the cash-strapped Kerala government has decided to stop paying employees for accumulated leave to save about Rs 200 crore a year. It has also cut pension benefits.

The Cabinet decided late Wednesday that heads of government departments taking part in the strike would be suspended. The threat was followed up with the suspension of one officer and five employees of the Kerala Water Authority. Officials warn the number might go up. The government has threatened to sack private secretaries of ministers who keep away from work. Two employees have already been terminated.

Unfazed employees continued to hold huge demonstrations outside the state secretariat picketing all the four gates. Only one gate was open, and only about 200 employees reported for duty. "The strike is total and it will continue," said C H Asokan of a union affiliated to the CPM. "We will not be cowed down by threats of suspension." The strike is yet to affect transport and government hospitals.
