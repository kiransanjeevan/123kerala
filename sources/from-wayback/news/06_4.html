'Salary in March if there is money in tresury'

THIRUVANANTHAPURAM: The Cabinet, which met on Tuesday afternoon after the talks with government staff failed, has decided to deal sternly with the employees' agitation and to come down strongly against those indulging in violence in connection with the strike.

Chief Minister A K Antony told reporters after the Cabinet briefing that the employees' strike was not against the government but against the people. He also sought the moral support of the public for the actions that would be taken to deal with the strike. Asked if there was any possibility of further talks or a compromise, Antony added that the government could not concede to impossible demands.

Antony, who remained unflappable throughout the news conference, also issued a veiled warning by saying that the government employees would have to cooperate if they wanted their salary in March. Cases would be registered, and arrests carried out, if people try to indulge in violence as part of the agitation. Protection would be provided to staff who come for duty during the days of the strike, he added.

``Revenue collection has reached nowhere. The Government cannot take loans to pay salaries anymore. We have already done that twice since we came to power. I have no idea about what will happen in March. If there is money in the treasury, salaries will be disbursed. I have not given up hope of the employees seeing sense and dropping their agitation plans.

The next two months are crucial and SSLC exams are approaching. It would be suicidal to go on strike,'' the chief minister said. He said that the government was not for any confrontation with its staff. ``At the meeting, I told the service organisation representatives that once the financial situation improves, the government would hold talks with the staff on all issues. I told them that we would have an open mind while reviewing the decisions.

We cannot give promises that cannot be kept.'' ``If we wanted to defeat the staff, we could have resorted to tactics. If we had not got the sanction from the Prime Minister for additional borrowing, the treasury would have been closed on January 29 and salaries would not have been paid. The strike would not have taken place. But that is not how we function,'' Antony said. Kerala was on the brink of disaster and ever since the UDF government took over, it had been able to look after the interests of its staff alone, Antony said.

``We have not been able to do anything for the farmers whose income has plummeted, the workers or for any other section. But the government staff got their salaries on time. Twice, we had to borrow money to give our staff salaries. The uncompromising attitude of the government staff is unfortunate. The agitation, I would say, is against the farmers, the workers, the beneficiaries of the noon meal scheme and poor patients in Government hospitals. The staff, with their organised strength, have declared an agitation against the people.'' The austerity measures were part of an effort to save the State, Antony said.

``We need a change of approach. The outsiders' perception of Kerala should change. Red-tapism has to go. We have to bring in financial discipline. There are many people willing to invest in the State and we have to create an atmosphere conducive for investment,'' he added.
