Neela Akaasham Niraye

Storyline
Unni (Nawas) returns from the Gulf to his native village. He had left behind a village that was abounding in goodness and harmony. But when he returns with dreams of spending the rest of his life in his own land, he finds that things have changed. Friends have turned foes and communalism has crept in. The family of Moosa Haji (Murali), which consisted of Moosa Haji's wife Mariam, his daughter Amina (Rehna) and son Ummer - whom he had considered his own family, had been distanced from him by the appearance of a wall separating their two houses. But the wall is not there in Unni's heart. And neither is it there in the minds of Moosa Haji, his wife or Amina. But Ummer's mind has been poisoned. Gangan (Augustine) and Sivan (Saadique), the two brothers-in-law of Unni, are also against him. It is in this context that the love between Unni and Amina creates problems in the little village. Things are taken to a communal level. Events are magnified and distorted until a harmonious climax is reached.

Review
Here is a film that would better be termed 'a film with goodness in it' rather than 'a mere good film'. 'Neelaakaasham Niraye', produced by Usha Pookayi under the banner of Kukku Vision, does not dwell upon a theme that could boast of novelty. As for the treatment, it is an irrefutable truth that the makers of 'Neelaakaasham Niraye' have succeeded in doing their best to give a good film. Nawas has given a very good performance as Unni, while Rehna too puts in her best as Amina. Of course both Nawas and Rehna (who are getting married on October 27), are both comparatively low-profile stars in Malayalam cinema, but it is a rather amazing that both have performed like well-established and seasoned stars. Murali gives a good performance as Moosa Haji, while Saikumar too shines as Ummer. Of the songs penned by Vayalar Sharath Chandra Verma and tuned by Alleppey Jimmy, "Ariyaathe oorumen…" and "Thennal doothanaayi…" have turned out to be good numbers. Jyothi Kumar, who has jumped to the big screen from the mini-screen with 'Neelaakaasham Niraye' has done a fairly good job. The person who deserves the main credit, in addition to the director, is 'Ishtam'-fame Kalavoor Ravikumar, who has done a very good script for the film. In total 'Neelaakaasham Niraye' may perhaps not be termed a class movie, but it is one such film that would make us think about goodness at a time when virtue has almost vanished from our films.
