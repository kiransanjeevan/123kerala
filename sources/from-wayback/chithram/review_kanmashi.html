Review: Kanmashi

Ee Parakkum Thalika fame Nitya Das is appealing in the role of Kanmashi, while Vineeth Kumar as Vishnu is charming. Nedumudi Venu is his usual self and does his part well as Padmanabha Menon. Jagathy Sreekumar as Advocate Bhaskara Menon, Devan as Chandramohan, Varsha's father, Babu Namboothiri as Kanmashi's father and Kalabhavan Mani as Murugan have done full justice to their roles. The folk number 'Vala kilukkunna kunjole...' sung by Kalabhavan Mani is likely to appeal to his fans. Script by Alex Kadavil and V.R.Mohan is based on a story written by the director himself. Inspite of fairly good performances, good cinematography and beautiful frames, you get bored somewhere along the way. May be because you have seen such themes over and over again. Still, the film can be seen for the sake of the charming Vineeth Kumar and the mischievous looking Nitya Das.

Storyline

Kanmashi, tells the story of a young girl Sethulekshmi, who is fondly called 'Kanmashi' by all. She and her gang of little kids spend their time fooling around and getting rid of all the marriage proposals that come for heri. When Vishnu, a young textile designer comes to work in the textile mill owned by Padmanabhan Menon, things take a different turn. Kanmashi, who has been avoiding marriage and even mischievously fooling Murugan, her maternal cousin who is madly in love with her, falls in love with Vishnu. Vishnu too falls for 'Kanmashi'. But Vishnu has a definite purpose for coming to the place. And when the elders arrange the marriage between Vishnu and Varsha, Padmanabha Menon's grand daughter, matters become worse.
