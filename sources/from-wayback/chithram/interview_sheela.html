Interview: Sheela

Sathyan Anthikkad's Manasinakkare was not a mere comeback film for the dream girl of Malayalam cinema during the 70’s. Sheela is back in front of the arc lights after 22 years of self-imposed exile. Her contemporaries and colleagues say that she is once again back to where she really belongs. The new generation of actors are looking at her with awe and respect. As for the average Malayali, Sheela has revived the memories of a golden era in Malayalam film. She made her debut in films at the age of 14 in P.Bhaskaran’s Bhagyajathakam in 1964. She has directed films like Yakshaganam in 1975 and Shikaranagal. Sheela’s name figures in the Guinness Book of World Records for having been the heroine of the same actor (Prem Nazir) in the highest number of movies (117). The veteran actress in a candid interview:

Which was your last film? And why did you decide to take such a long break?

I think my last films were Spodanam, Thakilukottamburam and Madrassile Mon. Regarding my decision to stay away from the camera, I felt that life was becoming too dull and mechanical and I thought it was high time that I gave my family some time. Looking back it was the right decision and I have no regrets.

What made you think about a re-entry and that too after 22 years?

It was not an immediate decision, as my son always suggested that I do some good roles and it was at this time that a leading magazine wrote that I was willing to act. And to my surprise I started getting offers left right and center that it became difficult for me to decide and choose films.

How did Manasinakkare happen?

Actually I signed Viralthumpilaro first, as I liked the script. But It was Satyan Anthikkad’s Manasinakkare that released first, and I am happy about that, as Kochuthresia was very close to my heart. I thought that my Malayalee audience had forgotten me after so many years, but I was amazed by the way people accepted me when I saw the film with the public in an Ernakulam theatre along with my son.

So will you be doing such mother roles in future?

I don’t want to be typecast. I am looking for variety in roles and luckily all the films that I am doing have different subjects. I think that at my age, I can afford to be choosy and that’s what I have been doing. In my next film Akale I play the role of a Anglo Indian lady and Pritviraj plays my son.

As a veteran actress do you feel that film making has changed over years?

I don’t think so. The technical aspects have become far more superior, otherwise as an actress it is the same, start-action-cut. I have always been a director’s actress and still love to be so.

Any dream roles?

Yes, that of an AIDS patient. Remember that in Thoppil Bhasi’s Aswamedham, I had done the role of a leprosy patient, which sent positive messages to the society about the stigma attached to the dreaded disease. I feel that as stars we can do a little bit through our films and create awareness among public.
