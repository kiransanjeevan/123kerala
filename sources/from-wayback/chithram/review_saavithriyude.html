Saavithriyude Aranjaanam

Review
Harisri Ashokan is his usual self as Neelakantan, while Ashwathy doesn't capture the character of Saavithri. Innocent as Ravunni, Jagathy Sreekumar as Achu the headmaster and friend of Ravunni, Sudheesh as Dushyanthan, the young son of Achu master, are all their usual selves. Kalabhavan Mani has nothing much to do as Phalgunan. If the director had deftly handled the film, it could have turned out to be a very enjoyable light comedy. Looks like scenarist Govardhan and director Mohan Kupleri assumed that a mere adaptation of a noted writer's work without any creative interpretation from their part would be enough. Sorry!

Storyline
Saavithriyude Aranjaanam by noted writer M Mukundan, tells a story that takes place in a small village Thottumkara. Neelakantan (Harisri Ashokan) has borrowed money from almost everyone in the village and is deep in debt. He has pawned all the ornaments of his wife Saavithri (Ashwathy) save a golden 'aranjaanam' (waistband), which she is not ready to part with. The people of the village, continuously hound Neelakantan led by Ravunni (Innocent), a rich man. The villagers finally decide to make it a social issue. They convene a meeting to discuss the problem and solve it by arranging a wrestling match in the village to collect funds to pay off Neelakantan's debt. And this is how wrestler Phalgunan (Kalabhavan Mani) reaches the village. In the meantime, Neelakantan manages to steal Saavithri's waistband and pawn it with Ravunni. This leads to various complications.
