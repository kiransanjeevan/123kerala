Review: Nammal

Nammal is a sequence of campus moments woven into a beautiful story. It is not just love and adventure that Kamal narrates. The campus has changed a lot from the seventies and eighties. Poetry and literature are not important any more in the campus. Reality is evident now. Life in times of globalisation has changed the new generation and they are live to the present day problems. Campus considers life important. Right from the friendly principal, to the automobile driver from the slum are all important in the film.

Nammal is a story about two good friends. Shyam (Siddharth) and Sivan (Jishnu) are orphans who study in a college during the day and work in the night for a living. They are the life and soul of the campus, and are also famous for ragging juniors. All the students mistake them for rich kids, as they are always jovial and happy. Reji is the enemy of Shyam and Shivan. The rivalry started ever since Shyam-Shivan group won the college union elections by beating Reji's group. A new principal Snehalatha (Suhasini) takes charge in college along with her friend's daughter Aparna( Renuka Menon). She is teased and ragged by the boys and when she complains to the principal about them, Snehalatha takes action against them. But very soon Snehalatha understands that the orphan boys are very hardworking and meets their guardian who is a priest (Balachandra Menon). She discovers that one of them is actually her own son. That is the twist in the tale.

Among the new face, Jishnu is flamboyant and is sure to go places, while Siddharth has to work hard on his acting skills. The new girl Renuka is cute and photogenic. But it is Suhasini as Snehalatha who steals the show. She has once again come out with an excellent performance, as she has an author-backed role. Mohan Sitara's music is melodious and at the same time a few peppy songs add to the mood of the film. The scriptwriter Kalavoor Ravi kumar and director Kamal have made a fun filled campus story with conviction.
