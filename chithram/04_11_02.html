Samvritha's ticket to B’wood?

 

From the time Malayalam actress Samvritha bagged a National Film Development Corporation project — the Hindi movie, White Elephant — the media went Samvritha berserk, describing it as her ticket to B-town.

But that is not the case, asserts Samvritha. “Other than the difference in language, it felt like working on a Malayalam movie. We shot in locations in Ottapaalam as the film is about a small village in Kerala, where a holy elephant lives and how the villagers try to protect the pachyderm from the outside world,” explains Samvritha. The actress is unsure the film will connect with audiences. “White Elephant’s been made as a Hindi movie, so the film-makers and promoters are targetting a wider audience. It’s unlikely that audiences outside Kerala will relate to the story,” she says, adding, “The film’s for multiplex audiences and not much of a commercial venture; it’s meant for film festivals.”

But Samvritha has no regrets having worked on the film. “It was a good experience. Apart from brushing up my Hindi skills, it also gave me the opportunity to work with an organised crew, which planned everything in advance. We got the script and dialogues much before shooting began, very unlike working in Malayalam, where sometimes a scene is written once the artiste is on location,” she says.

 
 





 
 
Though she is interested in Bollywood and other south regional films, Samvritha is content doing Malayalam movies. “I did one film each in Tamil and Telugu some years ago and never went back because the offers I got thereafter were nothing to talk about. In Malayalam, I’ve been getting a mix of commercial and so-called art films. So, I get to toggle between not doing much other than smiling and romancing the hero and serious stuff,” she explains.
