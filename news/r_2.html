‘Pass orders that can be enforced’

Kollam, Dec 23: Chief Justice of India Justice S P Bharucha today asked the courts not to pass orders that could not be enforced in public interest petitions. Inaugurating the five-day National Legal Workshop and a seminar here, he said such orders would put the judiciary into dispute with people and would give room for citizens to react as “there is an order that a court had passed, but nothing has happened on that”.

More than 80 per cent of the judges in this country were honest and incorruptible, he said, adding a small percentage of “corrupt” judges brought “disrepute” to the entire judiciary. These individual cases should be investigated and they should be dismissed from service, he said. The 127th report of the Law Commission in 1998 recommended that the state immediately increase the present ratio from 10 judges per million of population to at least 50 judges per million of population within the next five years, he pointed out.

“But in the country, the ratio of judges in 2001 is 12 or 13 per million now,” he said. It was because the states did not provide necessary funds required to increase the judge strength and every state had stated that it had no more money for the judiciary, he said. It was the obligation of the State to provide justice to its citizens but it was not being honoured as was evident from the fact that the jails in the country were overcrowded and a large portion of the occupants were undertrials, he said.

Justice Bharucha said amendments to the criminal procedure code and the civil procedure code and the institution of fast track courts were intended to speed up the delivery of justice and consequently reduce arrears in courts. “But they are not the real cure to the problem of arrears in the disposal of cases by courts,” he said. The principal cause of arrears lay not with the judges nor with the bar but with the state government, he said. D V Subha Rao, Bar Council of India chairman, presided. Justice B N Sree Krishna, Chief Justice of Kerala, released the souvenir.
