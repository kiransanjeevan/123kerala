Indian Telephone Card gains popularity

Kochi: The India Telecom Card (ITC) service has become popular, registering a 300 per cent growth in six months in the Ernakulam Secondary Switching area of Bharat Sanchar Nigam Limited (BSNL).

A K Saxena, principal general manager (Telecom) of BSNL, Ernakulam, told a news conference here yesterday that the service would be further popularised by engaging more number of agents for sales and by opening more public call offices (PCOs) for easy access. ITC is a pre-paid service with the cards available in the denominations of Rs 105, 210, 525, 1,050 and 2,100.

Under this service, the customer can make all types of call (Local, STD and ISD) from any telephone without that telephone being metred. The call charges will be debited to the card only. Saxena said the Wireless in Local Loop (WLL) mobile service was also getting a favourable response. The service now available within two to four km radius of three exchanges in the city would be extended to all parts of the city by next month.

Similarly, the Fixed Wire Terminal (FWT), which is a replacement for landline for all practical purpose, has also become very popular with the available 2,000 connections sold out by November last. BSNL proposes to extend the service, having a range of more than 20 km from the base trans-receivers, to 12 rural centres by next month.

To a question, he said 30,000 telephone connections had been provided in the Ernakulam secondary switching area comprising Ernakulam and Idukki districts in the last financial year. The Ernakulam secondary switching area expects a revenue of Rs 300 crore during 2001-2002 against a target of Rs 330 crore. The fall is due to increased competition and development. Revenue in the last fiscal was Rs 280 crore.
