Going back in time

With many firsts to its credit, ‘Mazha Peyyumbol’ (When the Rain Falls) - finished on a meagre budget, without artificial lighting reflectors, track and trolley, crane and in a record 11 days - unfolds the saga of a Keralite who was in Pakistan during the time of Partition.

The film, by journalist-turned-director K J Abraham Lincoln, which boasts of many peculiarities, incurred less than Rs 10 lakhs for producer M Rasheed Perinthalmanna. “When films made with millions of rupees without proper planning fall flat and when soft-porn is invading and polluting Malayalam cinema, this is an earnest effort by a group of newcomers with a somewhat research-oriented mind,” claim the producer-director duo.

When Rasheed disclosed his plans about the film, Lincoln suggested many names but he preferred to entrust Lincoln with the project while considering his potential and long-standing experience of 23 years in filmdom.

Talking about the project Lincoln said, “It was Rasheed mainly who inspired me to do this. We two, and the rest of us involved with this film, make a perfect team. From my experience as a film journalist, I feel that very rarely do all the people associated with a film stick together as a team once the film starts shoot. But we remained a team when the film was being shot and still remain very friendly, like members of a family, even after the film has been made.” “Not a single person involved in our film was after money. They were all dedicated artistes. Of courses, some were inexperienced and unprofessional, but it is the dedication that counts,” adds Lincoln.

‘Mazha Peyyumbol’ showcases the story of Baputty, who was on the other side at the time of Partition. When he decides to return home and settle down in his native place in Kerala, things start going wrong. He is even accused of being a Pakistani spy. But Baputty braves all odds and lives in his own place, giving love to all near and dear ones. In the process, Baputty had to face many problems. When his mother dies, he and his mentally retarded younger brother are thrown out of their house by a rich moneylender to whom Baputty’s father, a freedom fighter, owed money. But Baputty fights all odds and lives for his brother as well as for his childhood love Fathima, who has been married to someone else, then widowed and has two children. Baputty later marries her...
