KSRTC will not survive a strike: Minister

THIRUVANANTHAPURAM: In the wake of reports that Kerala State Road Transport Corporation employees are planning to join the indefinite strike by government employees, Transport Minister K B Ganesh Kumar on Thursday said such a move would be ''suicidal for the employees'' in the corporation.

''There will not be a resurrection for the KSRTC after an indefinite strike,'' the Minister told newspersons here. The KSRTC employees had every right to express their sympathy with the government employees. ''But the difference is that government will pay salaries to its employees, even if by borrowing money, once the strike ends. In our case, nobody will give loan to the KSRTC in such a situation. So if you decide to go for a strike, it will be suicidal for you,'' he said, appealing to the employees not to take any such decision. Stating that the corporation had already taken loans from various funding agencies, he pointed out that the KSRTC had to repay a loan of Rs 61 crore to the HUDCO alone.

Any strike at this stage would seriously affect the functioning of the corporation and its mere existence would be in doubt after a strike. He said the KSRTC was making an operative loss of Rs 15.50 crore every month when the United Democratic Front government assumed office seven months ago. However, due to a series of reforms introduced in the sector, the loss could be brought down to Rs 4.5 crore a month. There was a decline of Rs 12 lakh in KSRTC's daily collection due to the strike by govt staff on Wednesday. He said the corporation proposed to hold a series of adalats in all the districts to settle about 18,000 cases before the Motor Accident Claims Tribunal.
